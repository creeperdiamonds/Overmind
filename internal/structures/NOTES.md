# structures

Overworld, Nether and End structures (plan Tasks 9 to 11), our own Go from the study of BDS 1.26.60.6: `NOTES-bedrock-structures.md` (N1) and `NOTES-structures-phase2.md` (N2), outside the repo. Every constant cites its section. Nothing of Mojang's is in the repo: templates, loot tables and the data-driven jigsaw JSON are read at start-up from the install in `BedrockDataDir` (`LoadData`); without it `internal/app` skips structures with one log line.

## How it fits

- `worldgen` exposes what structures need (`structures_api.go`: seeds, stored biomes, preliminary surface, Bedrock's MT and Java's LCG). The Bedrock profile places a chunk's structures into its carvers stage through `PlaceLogged` (below); Overjava calls `PlaceChunk` after a chunk's own blocks are written (`SetStructures`, `GenerateColumn`). A chunk is still a pure function of the seed and its position (`TestStructuredChunksDoNotDependOnOrder`): a piece that crosses chunks is rebuilt from its start in each (small bounded caches of assembled starts change speed only; see Memory).
- Chests get `LootTable` and `LootTableSeed` block entity fields (N2 2.1: the seed is `nextInt()` of MT(the chunk's decoration seed), seeded afresh for each structure type (P17.2), drawn in placement order). The Dragonfly fork rolls the table on first open through `block.LootFiller` = `Data.Fill` (N2 2.2, 2.3).
- When structures go in (NOTES-structures-phase2 P21.1): BDS post-processes a chunk's structures in their own stage, before any decoration (its own or a neighbour's) reaches the chunk; per cell the order is terrain, the chunk's structures, then the decorations of its 3 x 3 in parity order, so features write over structure blocks and see them. In the Bedrock profile the generator writes each chunk's structures into its carvers stage (`worldgen/structures_base.go`, `PlaceLogged`; structures read the terrain only) and decorates from those structured bases; a structure block entity is kept when the decoration left its block, and dropped when a later structure write replaced its block (as BDS removes a replaced block's entity). Overjava keeps placing them after decoration.
- Order of structures inside a chunk: BDS's registry order (P18.4): villages, strongholds, the scattered feature (huts, temples, igloos, pyramids), mineshafts, mansions, buried treasure, monuments, ocean ruins, shipwrecks, ruined portals, ancient cities, each post-processing with the chunk's random seeded afresh (P17.2), so the order changes no draw, only which blocks win where two types overlap. Then the data-driven jigsaws (P20.5): one Xoroshiro128++ per chunk seeded with Java's decoration-seed formula on the chunk coordinates, their starts by decoration step (trail ruins and trial chambers underground_structures, camps surface_structures); every container block with NBT a template places draws nextLong(), a loot container keeping its low word as its seed: all 1,519 trial chamber and camp containers of the four measured worlds have their seed in their chunk's stream, 586 at BDS's position with BDS's seed, 296 of 395 in the chunks where our containers are BDS's (the rest: trial chamber layout). Inside one step BDS orders starts by its cache's hash (not emulated). Barrels, pots and dispensers keep the table in their block entity; the fork rolls only chests' tables.
- `NearestStronghold` (eye of ender): rings and 200-chunk grid (N1 2.5). It names the corner of the start piece (the spiral stairs down), which is built.

## Data

- `Data.Template`: Java structure NBT (gzip, big-endian), read by `worldgen/javatemplate.go` (shared with the fossil feature: at most 4 MB per file packed and unpacked, 256 blocks a side, every block's palette index and position checked; `worldgen/javanbt.go` grows lists as elements arrive). Newest vanilla pack wins. A template a structure needs and the install lacks is logged once per path.
- `javablocks.go`: Java block states to Bedrock 1.26 names and states, then the nearest registered Dragonfly state. BDS's own table is code, not data; names are what the tests compare, states are best effort.
- Own blocks for structures (`worldgen/structure_blocks.go`): suspicious sand and gravel (with their loot table and seed), the flower pot (with its plant), the dispenser (with its loot table and seed) and the mob spawner (with its mob).
- The Nether's structures are their own `Structures` (`New` on the Nether generator; `internal/app` attaches them with the same install data): the writer takes the Nether's 0..127, and the Nether's chunk goes through the same placement hook after its decoration.
- Code-built oriented pieces (`pieces.go`): P14.1's local-to-world mapping per orientation; facings turn with it in Java's frame (local north along +z: the ladders and stair runs of P14.4 read that way). Block names are what the tests compare; facing states (torches, doors, buttons, frames) follow Dragonfly's encodings and were not checked against BDS.
- Loot (`loot.go`): fill (N2 2.3) and rolls, tiers, entry pick, set_count, set_data, set_damage and the enchant functions' draws as read from the code (N2 phase 3), and NOTES-loot-trial-bastion T2's rules (validated there on 212 of 212 slots of 18 BDS chests, not re-checked here): random_aux_value draws, set_count is not clamped but cut into full stacks, the context conditions (biome_has_tag ...) pass (BDS evaluates them; the fill has no context), unknown items still run their functions and take a slot (fireball is fire_charge), and the draws of set_ominous_bottle, set_stew_effect, specific_enchants, random_block_state and random_dye (their effects other than the draws are not applied). The enchantment choice is BDS's thread random, seeded from OpenSSL (T2a): not reproducible; ours picks one compatible enchantment, while BDS's can give 1 to 3.
- Transcribed tables (owner's ruling 43): a structure's piece and pool tables read from BDS's registration code live in its own `pools_<structure>.go` (`pools_ancient_city.go`, `pools_village.go`, `pools_trial_chambers.go`, `pools_bastion.go`), each with a "TRANSCRIBED TABLE" header naming its source. The code-built geometry of the monument, fortress, stronghold, mansion, mineshaft and temples (`monument.go`, `fortress.go`, ...) waits on the owner's ruling (structures review r3-11, I1): extend ruling 43 to it, or re-express it as data tables. Templates, Mojang files and code never go in the repo.
- Provenance: `Data.Fingerprint` hashes every template path and size, every loot table's pool count, the `.mcstructure` set (bastion mob elements) and every worldgen archive file read (jigsaw structures, template pools, processor lists, structure sets) by its text, so a BDS update changing any of them warns at the seam.
- Loot limits: a table is refused when read if its rolls, tiers or bonus rolls pass 64, a count or level passes 1024, or a weight passes 2^20; one fill makes at most 4 entry picks per slot (loot is rolled on the tick).
- Terrain adaptation: `TerrainAdjustments` gives the generator each rigid city piece (feature elements included) whose box grown by 12 reaches the chunk; the generator adds the kernel term to every block's density after the squeeze and does not take its all-solid or all-sky cell shortcuts there.

## Reach

A chunk looks only at the starts within a structure's reach (chunks); a piece past it would be cut at the reach's edge, where BDS writes it whole. `TestPiecesStayWithinReach` assembles thousands of starts of every assembled structure (6 seeds, region candidates without the biome tests: the layout depends only on the start's random and the terrain) and checks every piece box. Read bounds come from the placer; the two placers with no distance limit are measured, with a margin of 2.

## Memory

Outside the generator's 30 MB cache cap, measured on BDS 1.26.60's install (`TestTemplateMemory`, `TestStructureMemory`):
- Parsed templates are kept for the server's life (parsing again per use would put a copy in every cached start: 64 cached mansions took 379 MB that way). The pools' 1,036 templates take 43.5 MB of heap once every pool is built; the install's whole set, 1,285 templates, 54.1 MB, the most `Data` keeps (a warning is logged past 64 MB). `templateBytes`'s estimate is within 3% of the heap.
- Every structure cache full (64 starts per kind, 16 assembled starts per dimension): mineshafts 1.1 MB, strongholds 1.3, monuments 0.45, mansions 4.3, ocean ruins 0.03, ruined portal plans 4.6 (8 measured), fortresses 0.7, end cities 0.4, assembled starts 0.25 per dimension: 13.4 MB in all.
- So at most about 68 MB beside the cache cap, most of it templates the pools need. The spider corridor marks (at most 4,096) are small.
